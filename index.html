The college admissions process should be exciting - a moment of introspection with an eye towards a future brimming with possibility and potential. Of course, if you ask the average teenager or parent, the first word that typically comes to mind is stressful. But it doesn't have to be. I've spent the past 20 years helping hundreds of families to navigate this transition with ease. My goal is to empower college applicants to recognize their strengths, find their unique voice, and embrace happiness as a fundamental criteria in plotting out the next four years of their lives.

About Josh

For more than two decades, Josh Eckhaus has served as a mentor, advocate, strategist, diplomat, and amateur therapist to families navigating the college admissions process. He's worked with families around the world, building a full-spectrum admissions program and helping applicants find their perfect fit at a wide range of colleges and universities.

Josh genuinely loves the individuality, authenticity, and creativity inherent in the college application process. As a writer, a former actor, an academic, and a lifelong musician, he is comfortable working with everyone, from painters and poets to scientists and soccer players.

USC adds a binding Early Decision option
Beginning with Fall 2027 applicants, USC will offer Early Decision across nearly all undergraduate programs, a binding Nov 1 deadline, with decisions about a month ahead of Early Action. EA and RD remain, aid and merit still apply, and Kaufman, Thornton, and Dramatic Arts are excluded. Read →
UC's testing decision pushed to June 2027
Faculty pressed the Regents to decide on reinstating an SAT/ACT requirement by September; instead the chair set a recommendation date of June 2027. UC has been test-free since 2021, and any change would still need Regents approval. Read →
Grade inflation vs. what tests show
Grades have climbed for three decades while ACT and NAEP results stagnated or fell, so report cards say students are learning more while tests say otherwise. New research finds lenient grading leaves students less likely to pass later courses, finish high school, or enroll in college. Read →
